Civic Technology is the use of digital tools and platforms that help people engage with governments and public institutions in more open, inclusive, and meaningful ways. It enables people to access information, raise concerns, share ideas, and shape policies. (“Civic Tech for Transparent, Inclusive, and Accountable Governance”) Civic technology can include websites, mobile applications, online surveys, digital forums, and other platforms that allow citizens to communicate with their local government. 

A smart city is an urban area where technology and data collection help improve quality of life as well as the sustainability and efficiency of city operations (Gomstyn). Smart cities use technology to address issues such as transportation, public services, energy use, safety, and the environment. However, the information used by smart cities does not have to only come from government systems or sensors. Citizens can also provide valuable information about what is happening in their communities. 

Smart cities can benefit from civic technology because it allows governments, non profits, and other organizations to collect data and feedback directly from citizens. For example, residents could use an online platform to report potholes, broken streetlights, and other issues. This information could help city officials identify problems more quickly and determine where resources are needed. Civic technology can also allow residents to share their opinions about proposed projects and policies giving city governments a better understanding of what their communities need. However, there is caution if this tech is available to only certain populations rather than the whole city. 

If made fully accessible to every group, including having accessibility options, different languages, and having areas around the city for anyone to be able to file an issue, civic technology can make smart cities more effective with citizen participation to their data. Smart cities can use information collected from a wider range of residents to better understand local problems, improve public services, and make more informed decisions. By combining technology with inclusive citizen participation, civic technology can help smart cities become more responsive to the people of the city.
